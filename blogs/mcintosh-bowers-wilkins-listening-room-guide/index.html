A balanced triangle is the starting point—not the finish line.

Bowers & Wilkins recommends beginning with speakers equidistant from the listening position, at the same relative height, and angled toward the seat. That geometry builds a stable stereo image. The exact final position still depends on the speaker model, room boundaries, furnishings, and what sounds balanced in the actual space.

What gets measured before final placement

The goal is not to force every room into one formula. It is to establish a repeatable baseline, then adjust while protecting symmetry and the manufacturer’s model-specific guidance.

  • Distance from each speaker to the main seat
  • Distance from rear and side walls
  • Tweeter height relative to seated ear level
  • Toe-in angle and clear sightline to the drivers
  • Floor stability and the correct spikes or pads
  • Doorways, walk paths, furniture, and daily use

Six layers turn expensive components into a coherent system.

High-fidelity audio is a chain. A weak decision anywhere—from room layout to equipment support—can limit the result. The useful conversation is not “Which brand costs more?” It is “How will every layer work in this room?”

Room and listening goal

Decide whether the room is for focused two-channel listening, shared family use, movies, or a combination. This changes placement freedom and system priorities.

Speaker choice and placement

Match loudspeaker scale to the room, preserve the intended orientation, and keep nearby walls and corners from becoming accidental tone controls.

Amplifier and loudspeaker match

Verify the exact speaker manual’s impedance and recommended amplifier power against the amplifier’s published output and connection guidance.

Equipment home and ventilation

Heavy amplifiers need approved support, required air space, practical cable depth, and a safe removal path for future service.

Power, signal, and control

Plan outlets, surge or power management, source connections, network needs, and control behavior before furniture hides the equipment.

Setup, measurement, and listening

Confirm channel orientation, polarity, levels, distances, and source behavior; then make careful placement and setup adjustments in the finished room.

The room changes what the same speakers can do.

Bowers & Wilkins notes that loudspeakers interact with nearby boundaries and that hard, reflective rooms behave differently from softer, furnished rooms. The solution is not automatically more acoustic treatment; it is understanding the room before choosing a response.

Listen for balance, not spectacle.A convincing system should hold a stable center image, preserve detail, control bass, and remain comfortable across the music you actually enjoy.
More bass is not always better bass.

Moving speakers toward walls or corners can increase bass energy while reducing openness, imaging, or speed. Follow the exact speaker guidance and tune in the room.

Room correction does not erase every placement problem.

Electronic correction can help, but it should follow sensible speaker and seat placement—not replace them.

A symmetrical drawing can meet an asymmetrical room.

Windows, openings, furniture, and reflective surfaces may make the two sides behave differently. Measure the actual result and adjust deliberately.
