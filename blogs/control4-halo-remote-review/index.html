Control4 remote comparison

Control4 Halo remote review and model comparison

Touchscreen or buttons? Compare Halo Touch, Halo, NEEO and SR-260 by how you watch TV, choose music and use the room. A working older remote may still be the right choice.

Published July 13, 2026 Updated: October 19, 2026By Denali Tech Team8 min read

The short answer: Halo Touch combines a touchscreen with media buttons. Halo keeps a non-touch display and adds a full button layout with a number pad. NEEO is touch-first with fewer hard buttons. SR-260 is button-first and uses Zigbee rather than Wi-Fi. Choose the interface that solves a real need—not simply the newest model.

Halo Touch

Check the documented software minimums
  • Halo and Halo Touch: OS 3.3.2 is the minimum listed in their data sheets.
  • NEEO: OS 3.1.0 is the listed minimum.
  • SR-260: its data sheet lists OS 2.7.0 as the minimum.

These are documented minimums, not a recommendation to install an old release or a guarantee that every controller, driver and feature works in every later system. Have the dealer confirm the exact controller, current software, remote firmware and supported source integrations before buying. Also confirm stock, accessory availability and any applicable service or subscription requirements.

Halo Touch: screen when you want it, buttons when you do not

Halo Touch pairs graphical selection with dedicated physical media buttons. Its aluminum housing comes in black or silver. It is shorter than the button-first Halo, but comfort is personal: try the grip and the controls you use most.

Choose it because you want the touch interface—not because a touchscreen automatically makes every task easier. Channel-number entry and frequent button use are worth comparing directly with Halo.

Halo: the button-first alternative

Halo's color screen is not touch-enabled. Physical buttons handle navigation, including a number pad and three custom buttons with digital labels. The housing is black molded plastic.

It is a sensible option when you want familiar channel and transport controls alongside a graphical display. Neither Halo model is automatically the better one-handed remote for everyone; test volume, navigation and play/pause in your normal seated position.

What the Halo voice button does

Control4 documents push-to-talk access to Siri when using a supported Apple TV integration and Xfinity voice when using a supported Xfinity integration. The active source and system setup matter.

A microphone button is not a promise that every TV, streaming app or smart-home voice command works. Ask for a demonstration with your actual source device, driver and software. Confirm any required accounts, internet access and services; other household voice integrations are a separate compatibility question.

NEEO and SR-260 do not have the Halo family's built-in voice-button interface. That does not prevent a compatible Control4 system from using a separately configured voice-control solution.

NEEO and SR-260: keep or upgrade?

Keep what works. If the remote is dependable and everyone understands it, age alone is not a reason to replace it. A battery, dock, connection or programming problem should be identified before you assume new hardware is the answer.

Upgrade to solve a specific complaint. More physical controls, a preferred touch interface, supported voice functions or a better fit in the hand may justify changing models. A different remote does not, by itself, fix an unreliable source device or network.

Make the transition gradually. When practical, keep the familiar remote available until the replacement has been configured and tested with the room's actual equipment.

Match the remote to your habits

Channel numbers and DVR

Compare Halo's and SR-260's physical layouts. Check the actual channel, guide and recording commands your source supports.

Favorites and scenes

Compare Halo Touch or NEEO when on-screen selection feels natural. Keep the useful choices near the first screen.

Shared or guest rooms

Clear source names and predictable controls matter more than the model tier. Try the daily actions with the people who will use the room.

Wi-Fi, Zigbee and charging are part of the decision

Halo and Halo Touch use 2.4 or 5 GHz Wi-Fi; NEEO uses 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. Check coverage at the sofa, bed and charging location, not only next to the access point.

SR-260 uses Control4 Zigbee. Its connection needs a healthy Control4 Zigbee network rather than a Wi-Fi login. That does not make the rest of the system independent of its controller, network or internet-dependent services.

Put the dock where you will use it. A reachable, powered charging location is more useful than a hidden one that nobody uses. Battery condition and use patterns affect runtime; the larger mAh figure alone is not a promise of longer daily operation.

Expect clear, predictable daily controls

Before calling the room finished, try starting your normal source, adjusting volume, navigating, pausing playback and turning the room off. Source names, favorites and custom actions should make sense to the household. Ask what “Room Off” will turn off, especially if equipment is shared with other rooms.

Before you buy

  • Identify the system: the controller, software and current remote model.
  • Name the problem: missing buttons, difficult touch navigation, short battery life, unreliable connection or unclear programming.
  • Check the sources: the exact TV, streamer, cable box and supported voice functions—not just the logos in a product illustration.
  • Confirm the complete proposal: remote, required accessories, programming, any system changes and the support included.
